# Canon & Discipline

*The most important page for the archivist. Read it before writing any session
note.*

## The three sources of truth

A fact is only a fact if it comes from one of these:

1. **The session transcript** (the WhisperX output in the episode's `transcript/`
   folder) - the only valid source for **what happened**, who said what, and any
   dialogue or inner thought.
2. **The `characters/` and `npcs/` pages** - the only valid source for character
   and NPC backstory.
3. **The `world/`, `locations/`, and `factions/` pages** - the only valid source
   for setting lore.

If something is not in one of those, it is **not** a fact. Do not add it.

## The six anti-fabrication rules (apply while writing, per scene)

These exist because it is very easy, while writing fluent prose, to drift into
invention. Apply them as you go, not just at the end.

1. **Presence ledger.** Before writing a scene, list who is actually on tape for
   it (by speaker). Only those people can speak or act on the page. If a character
   was elsewhere, say so in one sentence and keep them off the page - don't have
   them react or watch.
2. **Cite every quoted line.** Every line of spoken dialogue must trace to a line
   in the transcript. If you can't point to it, it doesn't go in. (Inner thought
   is the only exception - see below.)
3. **No forward knowledge.** Write each moment in the order it happened. Don't
   have a character pre-plan or foreshadow something the transcript shows them
   being surprised by. Their unpreparedness *is* the scene.
4. **Preserve improvisation.** If a player was winging it or fumbled and
   corrected themselves, write the fumble. Don't sanitise an ad-lib into a smooth,
   planned line.
5. **Default to omission, never invention.** When a specific is missing - a name,
   a food, a brand, an NPC's reaction - leave it out or generalise. *"The
   bartender"* beats a made-up name. *"Food on the table"* beats a made-up dish.
   Leaving a gap is the correct move.
6. **Expansion ≠ invention.** You may enrich *sensory atmosphere* already implied
   (lighting, smell, body language) and paraphrase inner thought from a player's
   own words. You may **not** add events, dialogue, named NPCs, or characters who
   weren't there.

## NPC names

Only name an NPC if the GM named them on tape. Otherwise use their role - *"the
guard,"* *"the dock-hand."* The same default-to-omission rule.

## When the transcript is unclear

WhisperX isn't perfect. If a stretch is garbled, note the gap in the summary
(*"a few minutes here are lost to the recording"*) rather than guessing, and ask
the GM if it mattered.

## When in doubt

Ask the GM. A surfaced gap is always better than a confident invention.
