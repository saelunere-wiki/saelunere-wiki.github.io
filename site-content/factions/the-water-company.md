# The Water Company

Undoubtedly one of the most important guilds in the city, the Water Company
strives to remain neutral amid the factionalism of [[The City Council]], in order
to ensure that the population and industry which need a steady supply can receive
it. Nonetheless, it is a thankless job, given the scarcity of large volumes of
clean water in the current age.

- **Director:** [[Samuel Greaves]]

## The Spire

The Water Company's tower is not a spire so much as a **converted water tower** - the same one that fed machinery in Parvo's factory days, kept and built onto rather than replaced. It has never lost the industrial look of its origins.

It is painted white, with **The Water Company** stencilled in red letters running vertically up the side, large enough to read from most of the district. The windows are small and the decoration is modest.

The exception is the top floor, glazed the whole way round. From up there the view is a full circle - across the city, over the curtain wall, and out to whatever the day allows you to see beyond it. It is generally assumed to be Samuel Greaves's office, or his home, or both.

## Formation

Rooted, like the Engineers, in Parvo's life before [[The Theocide]]: the Company grew out of the organisation that pumped water to and from the plant, including the heated water that served the settlements around it. Afterwards its members pivoted to supplying **clean** water to a city swelling with refugees.

Its neutrality was a decision made in those first years, and made out of fear. Withholding water from anyone would have been dangerous when everyone was desperate, and there were larger, better-organised groups about; so the Company resolved to stay **apolitical** - a balancing weight rather than a player in anyone's faction. The balance of power has shifted many times over since. The role has not.

## What scarcity actually means

For the poor, **getting water is as hard as getting food**. Clean water is bought, and what the Company supplies does not reach everyone equally.

The scarcity is specifically of *clean* water. Water for washing, laundry, and the rest is easy enough to come by, and nobody drinks it (see [[Water & Hygiene]]).

The alternative is to **boil it** - which is safer, and which means first finding water worth boiling. In practice that means **waste water**, and the business of collecting it is exactly as unpleasant as it sounds. It is ordinary, unremarkable, and quietly humiliating: a household that boils is a household that could not buy.
