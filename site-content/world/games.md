# Games

## Runs

A three-dice game out of the porter trade, played in pubs across the city and
usually for money. Joining a table is a recognised way into a social circle, the
criminal ones included.

### Origin

Runs began as a way of killing time in depot yards, where crews waited on a load
with nothing to do and nothing to do it with except the counting dice used to tally
a consignment. The name is the trade's own: a porter's **run** is a route worked end
to end, and the hand everyone is chasing is three consecutive numbers, one leg
after another with no gap in between.

It is still a porter's game. Other people play it, and well enough, but it belongs
to the crews: the rules come out of their trade, the words used at the table are
theirs, and anyone else who knows how to play learned it off a porter.

### Rules

**Kit:** three dice per player - a four-sided, a six-sided and an eight-sided - and
coins.

- **The hand.** Everyone antes and rolls all three dice in the open.
- **Runs beat totals.** A run is all three dice showing consecutive numbers: 1-2-3,
  2-3-4, 3-4-5 or 4-5-6. The four-sided die can never show more than a 4, so
  **4-5-6 is the best hand in the game** and nothing beats it. Runs are compared by
  their top number. If nobody has a run, the highest total takes it.
- **The reroll.** One compulsory reroll each, going round the table. You must pick a
  die and reroll it, whatever you are holding.
- **Dead weight.** All three dice showing 1 loses instantly, whoever's turn it is.
- **Ties.** Tied players each pick one die and reroll it **at the same time**, so
  neither sees the other's result first, until one of them wins.

**Table talk:** 4-5-6 is a *full manifest*, 1-1-1 is *dead weight*, and a player
forced to break a good hand by the compulsory reroll has been *sent back out*.

## Where it is played

Most tables at [[The Head of Steam]] are playing it on any given night.
