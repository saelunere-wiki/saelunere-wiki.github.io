# Madame Rooke

Runs [[Salador's Slip]], a bathhouse in [[The Stacks]], and lives and works on its
top floor. An older woman with salt-and-pepper hair, in a good high-collared
overcoat, with rings on her fingers and half-moon spectacles she pulls down to
look over. She is up by seven every morning doing the books and inspecting the
house, and she keeps a cat.

In that part of the Stacks she has a reputation as **something like a crime lord
who does not do much crime**. Everyone respects her and nobody wants to cross her,
because she holds a great deal of dirt on a great many people. In her own words,
she has *"a number of, let's say, friends who often need information on certain
things or people"*, and she procures it for them, for a fee.

She knows [[Mira]] of [[The Brassline]] - *"I know her, of course."* By Nell's
account she treats everyone who works for her well.

## The job she gave Lark

She knew how [[Lark]] had arrived at her house in the middle of the night before
Lark told her. Asked for a place at the house, she wanted to know where Lark was
from and what she had done before, and said that Lark did not look like a
cleaning girl.

Then she told her there are rumours that **a certain fallen nobleman's son** has
been seen at [[The Plumb and Bob]], named him as Aeska Volan, and said the
information is particularly valuable to some of her clients. Lark is to verify
that he is there, performs there, or is known to the people who drink there, and
bring back whatever more she can find. In return, a place at the house and a
reward.

She also told Lark she owes a debt for the intrusion, and that **if she wishes her
friend Nell not to suffer any consequences for harbouring a potential fugitive,
she will be particularly diligent**. She expects Lark back tomorrow at the latest.

## Lark's answer

Lark came back that night and **refused the job to her face**. She would not be a
pawn giving information to guilds, she said, and her life had already been
compromised by the Porter's Guild; she offered to leave.

Madame Rooke took it without heat. *"My dear, we haven't even agreed whether you'll
be staying here. This is to pay a debt of you already staying."* It was, she said,
**"purely a family matter between family members"**, and no concern of Lark's; the
people she works for are not necessarily his family, but they are searching for
him. If Lark will not do it, others will, and anything withheld becomes worthless
the moment somebody else tells her.

She needs value, not money. If Lark has a moral objection, she may bring **other
information worth selling instead - dirt on the Porter's Guild**, some underhanded
tactic or criminal venture. *"I will not sell this information to the bidder if you
do not give it to me about your friend Aeska, but I will need something to sell
instead."* The new deadline was the end of the next day. She would not give Lark a
second night.
